* glosary

- Involute: describes the shape of the teeth, the curves are involutes of circles
- Backlash:
  - space between the teeth
  - helpwith: preventing jamming, lubricant, fabrication errors
  - excesive is undesirable, especially if the drive reverses frequently
  - you can add more by making teeeth smaller or putting them apart
  - add on both gears, unless one is too small
- Pulley: for a timing belt
- Shaft: engine pin
- Boss: lump on the side (for engines)
- Axial Thrust: sideways pushing *force* parallel to the turning shaft

- Pitch Circle: roughly in the middle of the teeth
- Pressure angle:
  - angle between tooth with the ~pitch circle~
  - +angle => +strength +power +wear +noise +
  - new industry standard is 20°, old one is 14.5°
- Circular Pitch:
  - distance along the ~pitch circle~ from start of one tooth to the start of the next one
  - = PI * d / N
    - d is the diameter of the ~pitch circle~
    - N is the number of teeth on gear
- Module
  - = d / N
    - diameter of the gear
    - N is the number of teeth on gear
- Clearance
  - extra space at the tooth *valley* that separate teeths
  - allow
    - teeth to fit together
    - space for lubricant
  - sometimes a negative one is needed
    - ex: with large (>55) pressure angles

* trivia

- 2 gears invert the rotation
- 2 gears mesh if
  - have same teeth size
  - identical pressure angles

* types

- Spur:
  - no axial thrust
- Antibacklash: two gears tied together by springs (for instruments)
- Pin Gears: work on angle, slow (for clocks)
- Rack Gear: straight or on angle
  - locks:
    - with a worm gear
    - spring loaded matching rack, acting as a lock
    - pin
- Worm Gear:
  - made like from a threaded rod
  - tilted against other gears
  - for light loads
  - great reduction
  - other gears cannot move it (self locking)
- ring/internal/planetary
  - can be stacked
  - good reduction (movement source is the shaft)
  - for wind turbines (disc is movement source)
  - mating gear must have few enough teeth so that don't interfere on the other side of the ring
    - but enough to not interfere/touch besides the involute part
- bevel gears:
  - teeths
    - on the surface of an imaginary cone (the *pich cone*)
    - shrink as they get closer to the center of the gear
    - types:
      - straight:       spiral_angle=0  cutter_radius=0
      - spiral/helical: spiral_angle>0  cutter_radius>0
      - zero (angle):   spiral_angle=0  cutter_radius>0
  - pitch diameter == base of the pitch cone
  - for dealing with intersecting gear shafts
    - eg: to rotate 90° the rotation
  - small load
- angle/helical
  - more contact area
  - more load
  - smoother
  - quieter/less vibration
  - cons: some sliding (aka axial thrust)
  - change 90° rotation
  - two gears need to have oposite angles to mesh
    - eg: 35° and -35°
  - uses: for hand disc cutters, cars (differential, gearbox)
- herringbone
  - v-shape, cancel sideways force of bevel gears (aka axial thrust)
- differential gears:
  - on cars
  - for calculations (!?) in early "computers"
